Facts that the rules state without a version (for §B, §C or §E):

- Hidden-tab timers in Chromium (§C): about 10 s after the page is hidden, timers are aligned to 1 s wake-ups (`kThrottlingDelayAfterBackgrounding`). Intensive wake-up throttling (1 wake-up per minute for chains of 5 or more timers, after 30 s of silence, no WebRTC) starts after 1 minute for pages that finished loading, and after 5 minutes for pages still loading (Chromium `main` `features.h`, verify/06 and verify/07 corrected the notes' "5 minutes"). An open WebSocket does not exempt a page. Intensive throttling dates from Chrome 88. Rule: LIFE-06.
- Energy Saver freezing (§C): from Chrome 133, with Energy Saver on, Chrome freezes a browsing context group that is hidden and silent for more than 5 minutes and uses a lot of CPU; all tasks stop, WebSocket handling included (https://developer.chrome.com/blog/freezing-on-energy-saver, verify/06 and verify/07 "Missing but important"). Rules: LIFE-06, LIFE-08.
- `unload` deprecation (§C and §E): Chrome's rollout reached 100% of page loads in Chrome 154 (stable 2026-09-22); `unload` handlers no longer fire by default unless the page opts back in with `Permissions-Policy` or the enterprise policy `ForcePermissionPolicyUnloadDefaultEnabled` is set (verify/03, verify/04, verify/07). Rule: LIFE-07.
- WebSockets and the bfcache (§C): from Chrome 149 (the blink-dev PSA says 148; web.dev says 149), Chrome disconnects active WebSockets when a page enters the bfcache instead of making the page ineligible, and the page gets `error` and `close` (chromestatus 5068439115923456). The enterprise policy `BackForwardCacheForWebSocketsAllowed` = Disabled restores the old blocking on managed desktops. On a `Cache-Control: no-store` page, WebSocket use is a sticky blocker (`kWebSocketSticky`), even after close (verify/17). Rule: LIFE-07.
- bfcache for `no-store` pages (§C): Chrome rolled it out to 100% over March and April 2025, with a 3-minute limit (10 minutes for other pages) and eviction on cookie or authorization changes (verify/03). Rule: LIFE-07 Avoid.
- `keepalive` limit: 64 KiB for the sum of all in-flight keepalive request bodies in the fetch group, not per request (Fetch spec, verify/07). Rule: LIFE-07.
- IndexedDB durability (§C): Chrome 121 changed the default transaction durability from strict to relaxed; Chrome's blog reports 3× to 30× speed-ups (verify/07). Rule: LIFE-09.
- Storage quota: IndexedDB, Cache Storage and OPFS share the origin quota (Chromium: up to 60% of the disk per origin); `localStorage` has its own 5 MiB limit per origin (verify/17). Rule: LIFE-09.
- `performance.memory` (§E): non-standard and deprecated in BCD; it reports only the JS heap. Use `measure-memory` instead. Rule: LIFE-10.
- Lighthouse no longer has a `no-unload-listeners` audit (removed in Lighthouse 12); unload listeners show in the `bf-cache` and `deprecations` audits (verify/03, verify/04). No rule cites the old audit.
